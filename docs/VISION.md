# Lauren — Vision

> Status: Draft (Round 1 of discovery). Sections are refined as decisions are made.

## One-liner

**Lauren is an AI video editor: you describe the edit in a prompt, and the AI edits your footage.**

## What makes it different

Existing editors (Premiere, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut, CapCut) assume the user knows how to edit — cut, trim, sequence, color, caption, mix audio on a timeline. AI features in these tools are add-ons to that workflow.

In Lauren, **the prompt is the primary interface.** The whole editing process — not one feature — is driven by natural language, and the AI performs the edit.

Lauren is an *editor*, not a *generator*: it works on the user's real footage. It does not synthesize new video from scratch.

## Problem

Video editing has a steep learning curve. People with good footage and clear ideas still struggle to turn them into a finished video because the tools demand editing skill. In the AI era, editing should be as accessible as describing what you want.

(Founder's own experience: weak at video editing, which is the exact gap Lauren closes.)

## Target user

**Content creators** (short- and long-form: YouTube, TikTok, Reels, etc.) as the first audience.

## Platform

**Desktop first:** Windows and macOS.

## Project context

- Type: Startup
- Team: Solo developer
- Budget: $0 (constraint that shapes every technical and AI-cost decision)

## Positioning vs. AI video generation

Generating video with AI is expensive and gives up control over real footage. Lauren edits footage the creator already has, so it is **much cheaper than generation** while keeping the creator's own content.

## Definition of success

People are actually publishing videos edited with Lauren — edited, not generated.

## Non-goals

- **Video generation.** Lauren never synthesizes video; it only edits real footage.
